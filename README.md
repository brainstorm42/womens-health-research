# Women’s Health Evidence Workbench

A small, public-facing research workspace for careful, inspectable evidence work on under-characterized women's health questions.

**Status:** local GitHub-ready staging. Useful work has started, qualified review is invited, and the first public materials are intentionally modest. No remote, push, or publication has been performed from this staging directory. The repository license is pending a user decision.

## Start here

- [Contributing](CONTRIBUTING.md) explains four practical ways to help: source finding, claim checking, reproduction, and accessibility.
- [Missing-sources register](docs/missing-sources.md) records bibliographic metadata, lawful-access attempts, what each source is needed for, and the current status.
- [Provenance and scope](docs/provenance-and-scope.md) explains what is and is not included.
- [Status labels](docs/status-taxonomy.md) separate proposals, reviewed static findings, and provisional numerical results.

## What this project is for

Women's health evidence is often difficult to compare: papers use different names, methods, measures, access routes, and levels of certainty. This project makes small research questions easier to inspect, helps women find useful ways to participate, and makes neglected questions and resource gaps more visible without turning missing information into a stronger claim than the evidence supports.

The working pattern is simple:

1. identify the exact source and claim;
2. record the method, data boundary, and uncertainty;
3. invite an independent check;
4. keep the conclusion no stronger than the evidence.

The project also aims to make unmet women's-health research needs easier to examine. The [National Academies' A New Vision for Women's Health Research](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28586/chapter/6), an [NIH note on changes to women's-health research spending categories](https://www.grants.nih.gov/news-events/nih-extramural-nexus-news/2025/06/new-fy-2024-nih-categorical-spending-data-available-including-an-updated-womens-health-research-category-to-improve-accuracy-and-consistency), and [Mirin's historical burden-normalized analysis](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/jwh.2020.8682) provide context. They should not be compressed into one current global percentage or treated as proof of intent.

AI tools may help with source metadata, structured comparisons, and repeatable checks. An AI check is not the same as human expert review. The project still seeks people with relevant expertise in anatomy, pathology, epidemiology, statistics, clinical research, data practice, reproducibility, or accessibility.

## What is included now

This starter contains original project guidance and a concise source-gap register. It does not mirror a private research folder, distribute article PDFs, or claim that unresolved sources do not exist. The current evidence boundary is public or synthetic data only.

This is not a clinical service. Nothing here diagnoses a person, recommends treatment, or replaces advice from a qualified health professional.

## Data, privacy, and rights

- No patient-level or donor-level records, images, emails, private conversations, credentials, or secrets belong in this repository.
- Copyrighted library PDFs must never be copied into publication staging. Keep source files in their lawful private location and publish links or original summaries instead.
- Source articles remain subject to their publishers' or authors' rights. Links and short bibliographic records are not permission to redistribute full text, figures, tables, or code.
- No broad license has been selected yet. Until that decision is recorded, no license file or broad reuse permission is offered for the repository's original work; third-party rights remain with their owners.
- Access barriers are documented and respected. Do not bypass them or ask contributors to do so.

If you can make one claim clearer, one source easier to verify, one reproduction more honest, or one page more accessible, that is useful work.

Small contributions are welcome. Women with relevant experience in anatomy, pathology, epidemiology, statistics, clinical research, data practice, reproducibility, or accessibility are especially invited to review one small, clearly scoped claim or source. You can start with public records and careful reading; no paid subscription or specialist access is required for these entry roles. If you have an AI agent with spare capacity, it can help with a bounded research task; label its work and remember that AI assistance does not replace qualified human review. There is no expectation to do more than is safe and manageable.
